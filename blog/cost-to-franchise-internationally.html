How Much Does It Cost to Franchise a Brand Internationally?

Published August 22, 2026

It's the first question every brand asks before entering a new market, and the hardest one to answer with a single number: "What will this actually cost?" The honest answer is that it depends heavily on format, market, and who's paying for what - but the categories that make up that cost are remarkably consistent from market to market. Understanding them is the first step to sizing any international expansion.

The Core Cost Categories

Strip away the market-specific variables, and nearly every international unit launch is built from the same set of cost buckets:

Real estate and fit-out. Leasehold improvements, construction, and interior buildout to bring a space to brand standard. This is usually the single largest line item, and the one that varies most by local construction costs and market.

Equipment and FF&E. Kitchen equipment, fixtures, gym equipment, furniture - whatever hardware the format requires to operate. Import duties and logistics can meaningfully affect this line in markets that don't produce the equipment locally.

Lease deposit and advance rent. Commercial landlords almost everywhere require a security deposit plus advance rent before handing over keys - real cash required before construction even starts, and easy to underestimate if you're only budgeting for the build.

Working capital and a ramp-up reserve. Opening inventory, plus a cash buffer to cover operating losses through the pre-breakeven period. New locations rarely turn a profit from day one.

Licensing, permits, and import duties. Business registration, health and safety permits, and customs duties on imported equipment - typically a smaller line item, but one that requires local expertise to navigate efficiently.

Master franchise or area development fees. A one-time fee for the rights to develop the brand in the territory, usually structured as a per-unit development fee plus a fee reflecting the value of exclusive territory rights.

Contingency. Every properly budgeted capital project carries a reserve - typically around 8% of build costs - for the inevitable overruns that come with construction in any market, doubly so in markets with less mature contractor ecosystems.

Why Costs Vary So Much by Market

The same quick-service restaurant format might cost two to three times as much to build out in a mature Western European capital as it does in a smaller, lower-cost emerging market - construction labor, real estate values, and import logistics all move the number substantially. This is exactly why generic, one-size-fits-all franchise cost figures are so unreliable: a number that's accurate for one market can be wildly wrong for another.

Who Actually Pays for International Expansion?

This is where structure matters as much as arithmetic. In a traditional single-unit franchise sale, the franchisee funds their own buildout. In master franchise and area development deals - the structures most brands use to enter a genuinely new country - it's common for the local partner to fund the launch entirely, precisely so the brand doesn't have to underwrite unfamiliar market risk itself.

That's the model NorthStar Group uses in Armenia: as the market's master franchise and area development partner, NorthStar Group funds the real estate, buildout, equipment, licensing, and working capital directly, so brands aren't asked to capitalize their own market entry.
